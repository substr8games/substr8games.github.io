When the defense case is strong, the corporate objective shifts from containing damages to resolving the dispute with maximum speed, finality, and cost control. Civil litigation allows a determined claimant to drag out even a weak claim for years, whereas arbitration compresses the process:

    Finality & Virtually No Appeals: Under the Federal Arbitration Act (FAA § 10), grounds for vacating a binding arbitration award are extraordinarily narrow. Winning in arbitration closes the matter permanently, avoiding years of costly appellate review and motion practice that a losing plaintiff could pursue in court.

    Compressed Timelines & Lower Defense Spend: Private arbitration uses streamlined discovery and simplified evidentiary rules, typically concluding in months rather than multi-year congested court dockets—substantially lowering outside counsel fees on a claim the company expects to defeat anyway.

    Confidentiality & Zero Public Docket: Even a meritless lawsuit generates a public complaint, press coverage, and searchable court filings that can invite copycat claims. Arbitration keeps the allegations, filings, and ultimate defense victory out of the public record.

    No Jury Unpredictability: Routing the dispute to a single neutral arbitrator eliminates the risk of an unpredictable jury outcome or prolonged pretrial disputes over summary judgment.

In short, regardless of whether the company expects to lose or win, private arbitration acts as a universal containment mechanism: if the company loses, it isolates the loss; if the company wins, it secures a fast, quiet, non-appealable dismissal without public docket noise or multi-year legal fees.
