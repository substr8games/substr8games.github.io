Federal Law — ROSCA (15 U.S.C. § 8403) & FTC Act Section 5: The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act makes it unlawful to charge a consumer for an online recurring subscription unless the seller clearly and conspicuously discloses all material terms of the transaction before obtaining billing information and obtains express informed consent. Under FTC enforcement precedent (such as In re: MoviePass), failing to disclose material limitations or throttling mechanisms on the underlying service—even if buried in general Terms of Service—constitutes a deceptive omission.

State Law — California Automatic Renewal Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17602): California law requires businesses offering continuous-service subscriptions to present all automatic renewal and material service terms clearly and conspicuously before the contract is fulfilled, capture separate affirmative consent, and provide a retainable acknowledgment of those terms.

Contract Law (Duty to Disclose Material Terms): Under general contract principles, a party offering a standardized adhesion contract cannot enforce undisclosed, arbitrary conditions that strip away the core benefit of the bargain, nor can a seller rely on vague disclaimers to conceal concrete usage caps that contradict how the product is marketed.
