Whether a member of the public subscribes directly on the web (one.google.com or gemini.google.com), through the Google Play Store on Android, or via in-app purchase on iOS, access to the paid consumer product is strictly conditioned on accepting Google's uniform Terms of Service and Google One / Gemini Additional Terms. There is no "no-arbitration" retail tier or alternative checkout path:

    No Alternative Consumer Checkout: Consumers cannot negotiate individual contract terms, purchase a retail subscription over the counter without accepting the online Terms of Service, or pay a higher fee to preserve their right to a civil jury trial.

    Post-Purchase Opt-Out Only (Not an Alternative Path): The only way a U.S. consumer subscriber can avoid the arbitration clause is to first accept the contract, pay for the service, and then proactively submit a written opt-out notice within 30 days of initial acceptance. Missing that 30-day window locks the account into arbitration permanently.

    Enterprise / API Channels Are Separate Products: While Google offers commercial contracts (such as Google Cloud Vertex AI or enterprise Google Workspace agreements) that resolve disputes in Santa Clara County courts rather than consumer arbitration, those are distinct B2B developer and enterprise platforms—not public consumer access to the retail Gemini subscription product.

In short, for any ordinary member of the public buying the consumer product in the U.S., accepting an adhesion contract with a default arbitration mandate is the sole point of entry.
